# Open Take Studio — creative experience brief

The acceptance bar is an extraordinary creative product experience, not a passing build alone. The studio should be visually arresting, immediately usable, tactile, fluid, and worth returning to. This priority applies to arrival, editing, generation, comparison, branching, restoration, export and mobile. Preserve it in subsequent passes.

The November 3 First Impression assignment makes delivery part of that creative standard: real posters should arrive immediately, eligible visible examples should move without hover, and each source-matched invitation should preserve the exact creative material into its next tool. Prismatic is the moment-to-ending journey; Liquid Daylight is the object-to-camera/frame journey; Voices is the selected-sound-to-timed-motion journey. All three now have actual browser generation/edit, playback, download and saved-state proof; the portable film also played with the studio stopped. The brief's supplied public Higgsfield findings inform responsive posters, decoded-frame continuity and example-to-recreate handoff. These established patterns are adapted to original Open Take productions, with no competitor assets or invented popularity. See [first-impression validation](first-impression-validation.md) for evidence, exact measurement conditions and any remaining final performance checks.

## Identity and experience

Open Take Studio is independent of EF brands. Its visual material is the creator's work: full-color photographs, illustration and playable film. Near-black surfaces and bright neutral sans-serif type form the stage; coral marks intent and selection. Avoid olive/cream identity, italic serif heroes, abstract postcards, generic glowing dashboard cards and fabricated community proof.

Arrival pairs a short invitation and a working prompt/upload with actual media. Starting opens an automatically saved draft; names and organization can follow. Curate the first viewport rather than showing every duplicate take. Returning creators see their own work and can restore their draft and selection immediately.

Make relationships visible: example → workspace, video moment → extracted frame, frame → alternative clips, preferred clips → branching film. Motion explains these transitions; reduced motion removes travel without removing state or feedback. Hover/focus never generates or plays audio. A portable film should be as carefully presented as the editor.

All creative experiments preserve original takes. Undo restores the previous draft or edit; it must never imply reversing a paid, already submitted job. Preflight, source history and model details are contextual, not the primary visual identity.

## Review rubric

1. First viewport: actual expressive media and an obvious action, with no naming gate.
2. Direct manipulation: large media, keyboard/touch scrubbing, visible selection, contextual next steps.
3. Continuity: source moment and branches remain understandable, previous versions remain available.
4. Generation: real queue states, responsive navigation, explicit paid submission only.
5. Return: saved projects, selected take and draft survive reload; state restoration does not trigger generation.
6. Share: selected-path MP4 and portable film package contain real media and work independently of the app.
7. Accessibility: clear focus, sufficient contrast, responsive controls, no surprise audio, reduced motion.

Validation evidence and any remaining limits belong in `validation.md`; visual ambition is not a claim that every browser, device or access need has been tested.
